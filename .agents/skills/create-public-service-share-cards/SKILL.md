# Create a public-service share card

Read the guide, its cited records and the [editorial playbook](../../../docs/topic-playbook.md). Follow the [publisher release check](../../../docs/topic-playbook.md#verify-the-publishers-release) for any figure used on the card.

## Check the fact and answer before designing the card

1. Choose a useful fact a reader can pass on. Record its source and the exact table, passage or finding. For a figure, check the measure, unit, population, geography, period and publication status. For a rule or target, check when it applies and call it a rule or target on the card.
2. Write the proposed question and a short answer with its main qualification. Link the passage or chart in the guide that gives readers that answer. Check that the evidence covers the same service step, people, place and time as the question.
3. Match the evidence to the question.
   - **Is it getting better?** Check a comparable series across the period the question implies. Account for reporting breaks, seasonal changes and revisions. Describe a short-term movement at that scale.
   - **How well is it working?** Check observable delivery or outcomes against a relevant standard or baseline, or use a documented assessment with clear scope.
   - **What is being done about it?** Name the body responsible and verify the dated response and its implementation status. Any claim about impact needs outcome evidence.
4. Advance the card once both the fact and the answer are supported. If the guide lacks an answer, verify and add one through the [drafting workflow](../draft-public-service-topic/SKILL.md), choose a question the guide already answers, or hold the card with the missing evidence recorded.

## Prepare and review the card

Use one prominent fact and a short, open question that leads naturally to the answer. Keep the scope, period and source legible alongside the fact at thumbnail size. Use a recognisable image that suits the subject and reflects what the evidence establishes. Use the guide title for the link preview and include the fact and its qualification in text as well as in the image.

Hand off the proposed copy, the answer and guide anchor, and the source or dataset reference for [independent review](../review-public-service-topic/SKILL.md). Recheck the fact and question whenever the underlying release or guide is refreshed. Regenerate the card if the change affects what it says.

## Worked example

The driving-test pilot pairs **10.3 weeks** with **Are driving test waits getting shorter?** The figure is the provisional October 2026 median from booking to test among people who took a practical car test in Great Britain that month. The guide’s answer compares that series with DVSA’s separate booking availability measure. Both fell in October, after moving in different directions between August and September. The card can lead to that answer, with the observation period and provisional status visible beside the number.
