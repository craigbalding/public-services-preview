# Editorial playbook for public-service topics

Follow a public promise through delivery and show what evidence would establish progress. A reader should learn how the service works, where it falls short, what that means for people and what to check next. Use the [tagging guide](https://craigbalding.github.io/public-services-preview/topics/prisoner-tagging/) as a worked example.

## Build the account around the service

Define the population and jurisdiction first. Draw the shortest useful path from the trigger to the result. At each handoff, capture who owns it, what they receive and what has to happen before the next step. Show why the service was designed that way where a decision, report or accountable official supplies the reason. Attribute retrospective explanations as such.

Open the page with three sourced takeaways on the purpose, the main weakness and what to watch. Put the finding, its figure or evidence gap, the consequence and the progress measure together. Keep the full process history, datasets, methods and scrutiny exchanges within reach from the main account.

## Choose measures that answer the reader’s question

- **Stage and unit.** Say whether the figure counts requests, people, completed actions or results. In tagging, a visit attempted within the target window, a tag fitted and monitoring started are three events with different meanings.
- **Population and time.** Give the denominator, geography, observation window and data release where available. A point-in-time backlog answers how many are waiting. An entry cohort followed over time answers how long they wait. Show rates or counts in context, and keep different populations identifiable.
- **Change over time.** Compare observations with a consistent definition. Label breaks, revisions, provisional figures and missing observations. Date policy changes separately. Identify whether the latest comparable data cover the period after a change took effect.
- **Consequences and outcomes.** Describe what a documented case establishes and how far it generalises. A recorded incident may reflect detection as well as behaviour. Describe the population, comparison, outcome, follow-up period and limitations of any effect estimate. Explain the evidence gap when a finding lacks a figure or a reasonable comparison.
- **Cost and funding.** Follow the costs through the same handoffs as delivery. Identify who pays for the direct service and who absorbs follow-up work. Label actual spending, funded plans, forecasts and unit costs by year and scope. Check whether figures overlap or use different populations, periods or cost bases before comparing or adding them. Say which costs the public record leaves out and connect spending to a useful result, such as the share of people who receive a working service on time.

## Track follow-up and delivery

Use records from committees, auditors, inspectors or regulators when they test a specific claim about delivery. Cite the hearing at the relevant timestamp, or identify the written record and question, paragraph or page where possible. Explain the challenge, the response and the check that would settle the point. Preserve any subsequent correction.

Track a request and an official commitment according to what the record establishes. Append a dated entry when information arrives, is incomplete or is past a verified deadline. Use the precision of the source for expected dates. Check whether a report, response or action has been published, then test delivery against the relevant measure and baseline. Date announcements that predate scrutiny, and name the limits of any attribution. If the public record is sparse, state what sources or period the review covered and what would move the finding forward.

## Draft, review and refresh

The [drafting skill](../.agents/skills/draft-public-service-topic/SKILL.md) prepares the topic, evidence and build. The [review skill](../.agents/skills/review-public-service-topic/SKILL.md) checks the draft independently against the original records and identifies any required revisions. A clean build checks structure and references. Editorial review checks what the cited evidence actually supports.

Handoff includes the topic path, changed claims, relevant source references, unresolved gaps and build result. Use the same handoff for a refresh, with changes since the last review and updates to the follow-up history. A future scheduled workflow can run drafting and review as separate jobs using this handoff. See the [topic format](topic-format.md) for the fields and build commands.
